# The Neighbours

OBS Studio is one answer among several
to "how do I record or stream myself and
my screen?" These are the neighbours,
and what each trades. Nothing here is a
ranking; the right tool is the one whose
price — money, complexity, dependence —
your situation actually wants to pay.

| Tool | Shape | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Streamlabs Desktop** | Built on OBS's own code, with themes, widgets and a storefront wrapped around it; free, with a paid tier | You want the overlays and alerts bundled and are untroubled by the heavier, more commercial wrapper |
| **XSplit Broadcaster** | Windows only; polished, commercial, with a free tier that watermarks and limits | You are on Windows, want a supported product, and the subscription or licence is a fair trade |
| **vMix** | Windows production switcher, priced in editions, aimed at live events with many inputs | The event is the job: multiple cameras, instant replay, a crew's worth of features — and a budget |
| **Meld Studio** | A newer, deliberately simpler recorder/streamer, free | OBS's desk feels like a cockpit to you and you would trade control for calm |
| **StreamYard / Riverside** | Browser-based studios you subscribe to; guests join by a link; recordings land in the cloud | Your guests are remote and non-technical, and installing nothing matters more than owning the files outright |
| **Camtasia / ScreenFlow** | Editor-first screen recorders, paid | The recording is raw material for an edited video, and the timeline is where you will live |
| **Your meeting app's own recording** | The Record button in Zoom, Teams, Meet | The meeting is the whole job, the composed feed is not, and simplest wins |
| **ffmpeg** | The command-line engine under much of the above | You want a script, not a desk: same capture, same file, every night, nobody switching anything |

OBS's own trade is visible in the middle
of the table: it is the one that is
free, unrestricted and runs everywhere,
paid for with a learning curve and a
do-it-yourself answer to every problem.
Its scene/source desk is also the model
most of the others copied, so time spent
here transfers if you ever move.

One honest note about the hosted rows.
StreamYard, Riverside and the meeting
apps hold your recording on their
servers, in their formats, under their
subscription. That is often the right
trade for a one-off. For a series you
intend to keep — a season of meetings, a
podcast's back catalogue — ask the same
question as chapter 11: where are my
files if the subscription ends, and in
what quality did they actually arrive?
